## Excerpt (meta description)

Airspeed (formerly Glyphic) records sales calls and acts on them: it writes MEDDIC fields to the CRM, drafts follow-ups and flags deal risk. $20M Series A led by DN Capital (June 2026). GTMLens analyst take.

## Analyst take

Airspeed is the clearest example yet of conversation intelligence moving from recording to doing. Gong's category was built on capturing calls and surfacing what was said; Airspeed's pitch is that the capture is now table stakes and the value is in what happens next. Its agents write qualification fields (MEDDIC, SPICED) into Salesforce or HubSpot after each call, draft follow-ups, prepare reps for the next meeting and flag deal risk. Forecasting is built on the same call evidence. The company, founded by two former Google DeepMind researchers and called Glyphic until May 2026, raised a $20M Series A led by DN Capital in June, for about $25.5M in total.

The commercial posture is aimed squarely at incumbents. Airspeed offers to run free until a customer's Gong, Jiminny or Clari contract ends, and it says Persona replaced Gong with it. It also imports the last six weeks of a team's Gong calls, so a switch doesn't start from an empty history. That is a sensible wedge. The hardest part of leaving a recorder is losing the archive and the habits, not the recording itself.

The caution is the evidence base. The traction figures — close to 200 customers in 20 countries and revenue up four-fold year on year — are company-stated, and so are the customer results on its site: 41% more outbound-generated deals, a 30% shorter sales cycle and dozens of hours a month returned to reps. None of these is independently verified, and case-study numbers are selected by the vendor. Pricing is quote-only. What we can confirm is the funding, the named customer logos and the product surface.

For GTM leaders: Airspeed is worth a trial if your problem is CRM hygiene and follow-through rather than call review — reps who don't update opportunities, managers forecasting from stale fields, deal risk spotted too late. Run the 30-day trial on one team and measure field completeness and stage accuracy before and after. If you are on Gong mostly for coaching and call libraries, the switching case is weaker. Airspeed's advantage is in what happens after the call.

The strategic risk is that "act on the call" is where every incumbent is heading. Gong's September Mission Callisto launch and Salesforce's agents both point at the same job, and CRM vendors own the system Airspeed writes into. Airspeed's room to win is speed and depth of the write-back, plus being model-agnostic (it exposes its data to Claude and ChatGPT through MCP). Being well-funded for its stage helps; being a $25M company against Gong's $500M+ ARR does not.

Verdict: Pilot for 10–100-rep teams on Salesforce or HubSpot whose real problem is CRM data quality and follow-through. Watch, rather than switch, if you are an enterprise Gong customer using it mainly for coaching. Revisit when Airspeed publishes pricing or independently verifiable traction.

## Best fit

- B2B sales teams of roughly 10–100 reps on Salesforce or HubSpot where CRM fields go stale between calls
- Teams running MEDDIC or SPICED that want qualification captured automatically, not typed by reps
- Revenue leaders whose forecast is built on CRM fields they don't trust
- Teams finishing a Gong, Jiminny or Clari contract that want to test an action-first alternative without losing call history

## Worst fit

- Enterprise orgs standardized on Gong for coaching, call libraries and enablement workflows
- Teams that need real-time in-call agent assist for contact centers (Cresta is the right shape)
- Buyers that require published pricing before evaluation
- Very small teams (under ~5 reps) where a free notetaker plus manual CRM updates is sufficient

## SWOT

**Strengths**
- Action-first product: CRM write-back of qualification fields, follow-ups, deal-risk flags and forecasting on call evidence
- Practical switching wedge: free until an incumbent contract ends, plus import of recent Gong calls
- Named customer base (14 logos, 8 case studies on its site) and a DN Capital-led Series A
- Model-agnostic: exposes deal and call data to Claude and ChatGPT via MCP

**Weaknesses**
- All traction and outcome metrics are company-stated; no independent verification
- Quote-only pricing makes comparison with Sybill or Spotlight harder
- Small relative to Gong and the CRM platforms it competes with for the same job
- Recent rebrand (Glyphic → Airspeed, May 2026) means a thin public track record under the new name

**Opportunities**
- Gong customers reconsidering cost at renewal, especially mid-market teams
- Forecasting built on call evidence rather than rep-entered fields
- Becoming the call-data layer for teams building their own agents on Claude or ChatGPT

**Threats**
- Gong (Mission Callisto) and Salesforce/HubSpot agents moving into the same post-call automation job
- CRM vendors owning the write-back surface and its APIs
- Free or bundled notetakers (Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Granola) compressing the recording layer underneath it
