Three weeks into Q3 and the pattern of the quarter is already legible: venture money slowed to a trickle, and the platforms went shopping instead. Five AI GTM point solutions were absorbed by platforms in roughly a month — and the two rounds that did get done tell you exactly what investors still believe in. Here is everything that moved between July 6 and July 23, with our read on each.

The tuck-in month: five acquisitions in five weeks

The consolidation wave we documented in The Great Signal Grab did not stop at the signal layer. The full sequence: HubSpot acquired Warmly (June 30, person-level identification), Zoom acquired Common Room (announced July 2, buyer intelligence; the transaction closed later in July), Reevo acquired Ciro (July 8, prospecting agent; company release only, no independent coverage), Attio acquired Inconvo (July 10, natural-language analytics, an acqui-hire whose standalone product shuts down), and Clarify acquired Seam AI (July 21, buying-signal monitoring, slated to become “Clarify Signals”). All five terms undisclosed. The pattern is uniform: platforms are not buying revenue — they are buying finished intelligence capabilities to embed natively. If you sell a single-job AI GTM tool, your most likely exit is now a tuck-in; if you buy one, underwrite the contract accordingly. The standing 2026 rules — change-of-control clauses, data portability, annual-maximum terms — have gone from prudent to mandatory in five data points. (Updated Aug 6: added the Clarify–Seam AI deal, confirmed after initial publication.)

What still gets funded: a brain and a demo agent

Two rounds cleared our bar this window, and neither is an AI SDR. Alta raised a $25M Series A (July 8, led by Sumitomo-backed IN Venture) for a "Company Brain" coordinating agents from prospecting through AI calling — on top of your existing CRM, not instead of it. Named customers (Snowflake, Deel, Atlassian) and growth figures are company-claimed. Sable raised $45M (July 16, co-led by Sequoia and 8VC, both taking board seats) for Aidan, an "AI employee" that runs live product demos by actually seeing and clicking the product in real time — the most credible attempt yet at the AI sales engineer, with Notion and Decagon in production (company-claimed). Full profiles: Alta and Sable. What VCs are funding is precisely what our Build-vs-Buy analysis said survived: orchestration layers and bounded, product-grounded jobs — not rep replacements.

The platforms shipped, loudly

ZoomInfo ran the most aggressive launch cadence in the category — a CLI (July 9), a self-scored agent benchmark (July 10), and general availability of GTM.AI across Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, Agentforce, and HubSpot Breeze (July 17). That campaign gets its own analysis in The Context-Layer War. Clay shipped five times in three weeks: an API and CLI aimed at coding agents (July 9 — the same day as ZoomInfo's CLI), MCP for reps (July 14), open-weight models in Claygent for cost control (July 16), and always-on Account Research Agents (July 22), under a new homepage banner calling itself "the infrastructure GTM engineers build on." All vendor-announced, all shipped. Clari + Salesloft launched Salesloft Conversation Intelligence (July 14) and, for the first time, claimed a shipped "Spring 2026 platform integration" — we've appended an editor's note to our six-month scorecard and will test the claim at the December re-score. Zoom, not waiting for the Common Room close, shipped Revenue Accelerator's AI wave (July 22). Quick hits: Gong landed in the Microsoft Marketplace with MCP interoperability (July 1); Microsoft's Sales Agent hit GA in Copilot (July 1); Salesforce unveiled agentic B2B commerce (July 21).

One date to watch

The MCP 2026-07-28 specification lands five days after this publishes: the protocol goes stateless, and the enterprise-managed authorization extension — already adopted by Anthropic, Microsoft, and Okta — is promoted to stable. That second part matters more than it sounds: centralized IdP-based access control is the unlock for the compliance-gated enterprise deployments every vendor above is chasing. It also cuts both ways, as SecurityWeek notes, by shifting security responsibility onto operators. If your stack strategy follows our agent-native playbook — protocols over platforms — this release is the strongest evidence yet that the protocol layer is maturing on schedule.

The scoreboard

Quarter to date: $130M across 3 venture rounds and 4 announced acquisitions (Warmly closed just before the quarter line), zero IPOs, zero shutdowns — live view on the funding tracker. The capital pace looks slow only if you ignore where the activity went: into M&A and shipping velocity. The market isn't cooling; it's concentrating.
