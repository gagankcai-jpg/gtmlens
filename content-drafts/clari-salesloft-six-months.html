When Clari and Salesloft merged in December 2025, the pitch was a unified "predictive revenue system" — forecasting brain plus engagement muscle under one roof. Six months in, we can score the integration against what has verifiably happened. The short version: the org chart integrated fast, the product hasn't integrated at all, and the company itself says unification is years away. For the 5,000+ customers (company-reported, ~$450M ARR claimed), that gap is the whole story.

What has verifiably happened

Leadership first: the combined entity — operating as "Clari + Salesloft" — appointed Steve Cox, a non-founder integration executive, as CEO; Clari founder Andy Byrne did not take the job, which tells you the board's priority was M&A execution, not product vision. The exec build-out continued through H1 2026: CPO and CMO in December, CRO and CTO in June, plus a stated doubling of R&D investment. The first tangible joint product move Constellation Research could point to was an early platform integration and an MCP addition — real, but modest. Meanwhile the company's own customer FAQ commits to platform unification only "over the coming years," and Forrester's assessment flagged "substantial technology overlap" and a merger that raises "more questions than answers" on the product side.

What has not happened

Three things worth naming precisely. No unified platform — Salesforce, HubSpot, and Gong integrations run as before, and the two products remain two products. No verifiable customer attrition — competitor content urges renewal-time re-evaluation, but no churn numbers exist that we'd print; treat "customers are fleeing" takes as marketing. No formal competitive counterattack — neither Outreach nor Gong has launched a migration program, which is itself informative: rivals are positioning ("independent, answerable to no parent suite," as Gong now frames it) rather than paying to poach, suggesting they expect the integration slog to do the work for them. One widely repeated figure — that ~40% of Gong customers also pay for Clari — is single-source and competitor-published; we cite it only as the shape of the overlap problem.

The analyst read

Mergers of this shape historically fail on the seam: two mature data models, two rev-rec surfaces, two admin consoles, one roadmap promise. Doubling R&D and hiring an integration CEO is the correct response, and it is also an admission of how long the seam will take. The strategic risk is timing — the platform wave of mid-2026 means Clari + Salesloft is executing a multi-year integration while Salesforce buys finished agents and HubSpot absorbs the signal layer. The pitch that beats them isn't a better sequencer; it's a suite that already works.

The buyer's checklist at renewal

Existing customers of either product: nothing has degraded — renew on merit, but negotiate as if roadmap promises are unpriced options, because they are. Ask for contractual delivery dates on any "unified platform" capability you're being sold, and keep the reported $200–310/user full-stack bundle pricing (third-party figures, unconfirmed) as a negotiation reference, not a fact. Net-new buyers: evaluate the products separately as what they are today — our Outreach vs Salesloft and Gong vs Chorus cards score the standalone jobs — and price the standing 2026 rule into every contract: change-of-control and data-portability clauses, because this category clearly isn't done consolidating. We'll re-score at the twelve-month mark; the testable prediction is that "unified platform" remains a roadmap slide in December.
